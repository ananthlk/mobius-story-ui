# Over-invested. Worse outcomes.

## The One Thing
> BHPF members staff like a PCP practice but produce health-system-tier inefficiency — same FTE per dept, dramatically worse outcomes on AR, denial, calls/FTE, and turnover.

## Setup
`admin-overview` showed the network's FTE profile. This slide pairs that profile with outcomes — and shows the gap is not effort.

## The Argument
A $15M BHPF member and a $15M PCP practice look identical on staffing. Outcomes diverge:
- **AR days**: BHPF {{ANCHOR:ar_bhpf}} vs MGMA {{ANCHOR:ar_benchmark}} (+57% longer; ~$4–6M working capital locked).
- **Calls per FTE/day**: BHPF {{ANCHOR:calls_bhpf}} vs benchmark {{ANCHOR:calls_benchmark}} — small orgs average 34/day, 4.7× below benchmark.
- **Denial rate**: BHPF {{ANCHOR:denial_bhpf}} vs ADS/SimiTree {{ANCHOR:denial_benchmark}} (3× benchmark; ~$8–12M/yr lost collections).
- **Turnover**: BH sector {{ANCHOR:turnover_bhpf}} vs health systems {{ANCHOR:turnover_benchmark}}.

The structural cause: 34 separate back-offices can't share intelligence, can't pool payer patterns, can't negotiate from scale.

## Expected Questions
- **Q:** Why is BHPF AR so high?  **A:** Denial-driven rework. 18% denial rate compounds days-in-AR.
- **Q:** Are these outcomes correlated with org size?  **A:** Mostly inverse: small orgs underperform on calls/FTE more than large.
- **Q:** What captures these as savings?  **A:** `admin-s4` (AI CoE) — denial→<8% target, AR→34 days, shared HR.

## What This Slide Does NOT Claim
- It does not assign blame to any individual member.
- It does not size the savings — that's `admin-s4` and `admin-s5`.
