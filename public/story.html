How to read this analysis

About BHPF
Behavioral Health Partners of Florida is a coalition of 25 community mental health centers — the providers carrying the hardest patients on Medicaid rates, in communities others left behind.
⚠ Data scope
CMS DOGE captures fee-for-service claims only. Managed care encounters, non-Medicaid revenue, and grant-funded services are not visible. Every insight is directionally strong — not the full financial picture.
Qualitative layer
Where claims data couldn't answer the question, we went to the source. Conversations with BHPF member CMHCs helped validate what the numbers suggested — and surface what they couldn't show.
Data foundation
Source
CMS DOGE — Medicaid fee-for-service claims, Florida only, 2018–2024. The full FL behavioral health market (~$1.1B baseline in 2019).
81 procedure codes
Filtered to BH codes reimbursed under the FL Medicaid fee schedule. Excludes per-diem residential and medical crossover claims.
Org classification
Segments — CMHC, FQHC, Specialty BH, Ind. BH, SUD — derived from NPPES taxonomy, FL licensure, and Medicaid enrollment records.
⚠ 2024 note
2024 covers 12 months. A small share of late claims may not yet be adjudicated. Use directionally.
What each metric means
Market share
Segment's share of total FL Medicaid BH revenue. Numerator and denominator from the same claims universe.
Revenue per beneficiary (RPB)
Total paid ÷ unique benes. Captures rate and utilization together. Shown as $ + % vs FL market average.
Service mix ratio
Ongoing community care ÷ front-end care (intake, crisis, ACT). Higher = more sustained treatment relative to acute episodes.
Panel commitment
Share of clinicians billing Medicaid in month M who bill again in M+1. Medicaid panel continuity — not employment retention.
Reading the signals
Green / above market
Above the FL volume-weighted average. Not automatically good — a high RPB on a small panel may reflect selective intake, not higher rates.
Red / below market
Below FL average. Often structural — orgs with broad, lower-acuity panels will show lower RPB even when running efficiently.
The market average
Volume-weighted across all FL BH orgs. Large orgs with flat rates pull it down. A floor — not a target.

Chapter 3

Four compounding problems

The displacement produced four specific, measurable problems — each one eroding revenue, margin, and capacity simultaneously. Together they represent $102 million in annual capturable value across the network.
Problem 1 · Revenue
Rate gap
32 of 41 shared HCPCS codes pay less to BHPF than to new entrant providers. $7.8M annually — before volume effects.
Problem 2 · Growth
Leakage
25% of patients enter via intake. Only 4.5% convert to ongoing therapy. Each 1pp improvement = $3.8M recovered annually.
Problem 3 · Capacity
Churn
35% annual turnover vs. 21% peers. 362 excess departures/yr. Each departure triggers a 90-day credentialing gap and billing blackout.
Problem 4 · Cost
Admin
Admin spend up from 18% to 24% of revenue since 2016. Days AR doubled to 84.5. Revenue per biller fell 24%. IT spend +46%.

Act II — The inflection

The inflection

Why the status quo ends here — and what the three futures look like

Chapter 6

Three futures — only one is viable

The status quo is not a stable option. CMHCs face three futures. The window to choose is 18 months.
Future 1 — viable
Transform
Build shared infrastructure. Capture $102M. Own the AI moat. The window is open now at economics that will not exist in 24 months. Organizations that move now build an asset that compounds for years.
Future 2 — mission risk
Get acquired
PE operators are waiting. They will offer liquidity. They will extract margin. The mission — serving the hardest populations — is the first thing that goes. New entrants don't serve crisis. PE won't either.
Future 3 — existential
Cease to exist
The 3.4% net margin is rate-driven and fragile. When rates stabilize, it reverses. Without the revenue base, the infrastructure crumbles. The patients who depend on CMHCs have nowhere to go.
The 18-month window
AI vendors are building healthcare-specific products now. After that: buy at market rates — or don't participate. The window closes in 18 months.
Act III — The transformation

The transformation

Three interlocking components — individualized plan, shared services, AI deployment

Chapter 7A

Individualized plan

Every CMHC starts from a different position. Transformation begins with a diagnostic — where are you on each of the four levers, and which one is your biggest opportunity?

The four-lever framework applies to every organization in the network — but the starting point and priority order are specific to each one. An organization with 84-day AR needs the billing lever first. One with 70% turnover needs retention first.

Key insight
Each CMHC receives an org-specific diagnostic: current position vs. network benchmark on all four levers, priority order, vendor stack analysis, and admin structure vs. AI-native target.
